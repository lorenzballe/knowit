Calibration

Say how sure you are.

Astute doesn't reward being right. It rewards knowing how likely you are to be right.

Every answer carries a number: 50, 60, 70, 80 or 90 per cent sure. Over the weeks, Astute plots how often you were right at each. Drag to read a typical first month.

Medicine

A test for a rare disease is 99% accurate. You test positive. How likely is it that you're ill?

About 9%. If one person in a thousand has the disease, the test finds them — and flags about ten healthy people alongside. Most positives are false ones.

Bar move

Before you trust a positive, ask how rare the thing is.

Technology

Face recognition is 99% accurate. It flags a face in a crowd of 100,000. How likely is it that they're on the watchlist?

About 1%. If ten people in that crowd are on the list, the system finds them — and a thousand who aren't. Same maths, different world.

Bar move

Ninety-nine per cent accurate can still be wrong almost every time.

The science

What the evidence says.

  1. 01

    You can't train general intelligence.

    Simons et al., 2016

  2. 02

    You can train away specific biases, and it transfers.

    Morewedge et al., 2015; Sellier, Scopelliti & Morewedge, 2019

  3. 03

    You can train calibration.

    Mellers et al., Good Judgment Project — about 10% better forecasts

So Astute doesn't promise a bigger brain. It trains the three things that move.
