FIRE IN ZERO-G

Why do flames on Earth have that teardrop shape?

Hot combustion gas moves faster and takes up more space than the cool air around it, so its buoyant force beats its weight and it rises. Rising, it pulls fresh oxygen in from below and stretches the flame up. No gravity, no buoyancy: the flame stays a ball.

Switch gravity to zero, then to hyper-g, and watch the flame.
