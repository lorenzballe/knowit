Why do Mercator maps make countries near the poles look so huge?

Drawn ×1.0 its equator size

Drag Wyoming north, toward the pole. Watch the gaps between latitude lines.

Mercator keeps every angle true, so wherever it widens a place east–west it must stretch it north–south just as much. Near the poles the latitude lines spread apart and both stretches grow, so the same land is drawn far bigger. That is why Russia looks larger than Africa, yet is really smaller.
