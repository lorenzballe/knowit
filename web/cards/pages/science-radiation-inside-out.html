Why is a radioactive atom more dangerous than its radiation at a distance?

Drag the Geiger counter away from the source. Listen to the clicks.

Out there it fades. Inside, it can't miss.

Radiation falls off fast with distance, and a lot of it can't even get through air; a little material blocks it. Breathe the atom in and it sits right beside your cells, firing straight into them.

WHAT TO KEEPRadiation far away fades; a radioactive atom inside you hits cells directly.
