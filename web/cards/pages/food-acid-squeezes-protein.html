ACID COOKING

Acid, no heat. What does the world’s strongest acid do to meat?

Tap how you think the meat turns out. It shows your guess.

It cooks it: dry and stringy, its juices gone.

Like heat, acids squeeze the meat’s proteins: chemists call it denaturing. Squeezed, they lose all the juices, and the meat turns dry and stringy, the way overcooked meat does.

WHAT TO KEEP Heat isn’t the only thing that cooks: an acid squeezes the proteins just the same.
