Why are 0 and 1 the only numbers that equal their own square in p-adics?

Arrows show where each digit lands when squared.

n² = n means n × (n − 1) = 0

In base 10, two non-zero numbers can make 0: 6 × 5 = 30 ends in 0, because 10 = 2 × 5. With a prime base that never happens, so n or n − 1 must be 0.
