WHERE DOES HE LAND?

Why did ancient Olympic long jumpers take off holding stone or lead weights in their hands?

Empty-handed he lands at 3 m. Now he holds 2 to 9 kg in each hand: tap the sand where he lands.

To jump farther. Swung forward at take-off, weights of 2 to 9 kg add at least 17 cm to a 3 m standing jump, and the best mass in the tests matched weights dug up at ancient sites.

WHAT TO KEEPBefore calling an old practice a ritual, test whether it simply worked.
