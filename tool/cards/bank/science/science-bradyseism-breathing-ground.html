What is bradyseism, and why are some ancient towns underwater?

Drag across the picture to run the centuries, from then to today.

This coast sits on a huge caldera, a supervolcano. As magma moves below, the ground slowly rises and falls over centuries and millennia. Here the coastal strip sank below the sea, and its ruins now lie 5 to 8 metres down.
