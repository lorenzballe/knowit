Swallow Teflon and you're pretty safe. Not so with the chemicals used to make it. Why?

Tap to grow a processing aid like PFOA to the length you think it is.

Processing aids are 5 to 10 carbons: small enough to get in

Teflon's chains are too big to be absorbed, so swallowed Teflon is flushed out. Processing aids like PFOA or GenX, 5 to 10 carbons long, can enter the blood. Their ends are usually acids; the most common, perfluoroalkyl acids, can bind to blood proteins and travel anywhere in the body.

WHAT TO KEEP A chemical can only harm what it reaches, and here size decides whether it gets in.
