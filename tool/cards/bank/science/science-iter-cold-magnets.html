FUSION

ITER's plasma hits 150 million °C. How cold must its magnets be?

Drag the marker to guess the magnets' temperature.

−269 °C

ITER's magnets are superconductors: only at that deep cold do they carry current with no electrical resistance. That is how they make the huge magnetic field that holds the 150-million-degree plasma away from the walls, since no material could touch it.

WHAT TO KEEPTo hold the hottest thing on Earth, ITER needs some of the coldest magnets.
