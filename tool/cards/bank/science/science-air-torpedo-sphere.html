Trapped air turns the ball into a torpedo

The researchers combined two tricks: the Leidenfrost effect and superhydrophobicity, a water-repelling surface. Together they trap air around a dropped sphere, which then slices through the liquid as a torpedo shape.

WHAT TO KEEP To cut drag, change the shape the liquid touches, not the thing inside.
